## What This LMS Should Do

1. Help you decide what to study next.
2. Track progress without making the vault heavy.
3. Connect learning to projects, products, research, and founder thinking.
4. Keep Obsidian as the source of truth.
5. Let a future app read the same markdown files instead of trapping your knowledge in a database.

## Learning Loop

Every topic should move through this loop:

1. Research: collect the important ideas, sources, standards, papers, examples, and open questions.
2. Learn: explain the concept in your own words and connect it to what you already know.
3. Build: complete an assignment, project, simulation, dataset, product sketch, or system design.
4. Reflect: decide what changed in your understanding and what to learn next.
5. Apply: connect the topic to a company, platform, regulatory system, industrial system, or personal capability.

## Personal LMS Levels

| Level | Name | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understand | Clear notes, definitions, diagrams, examples |
| 2 | Engineer | Working implementation, model, analysis, or design |
| 3 | Research | Papers, standards, evidence, unresolved questions |
| 4 | Founder Thinking | Problem, market, buyer, moat, product thesis |

## Daily Use

1. Open [[000_LMS/Current Semester|Current Semester]].
2. Pick one active learning unit.
3. Read or write in its `Research.md`, `Learning.md`, or `Assignment.md`.
4. Update status, evidence, and next action.
5. End with one short reflection.
